Sen or sedan in Swedish?

Short answer

Sedan means then / afterwards or since, and it works in every kind of text. Sen is an informal short form of sedan – fine in chats, not in applications. And sen is also a different word: late (Bussen är sen – the bus is late).

Sedan: then, afterwards, since

Först åt vi, och sedan gick vi hem. (First we ate, and then we went home.)

Jag har bott i Sverige sedan 2021. (I have lived in Sweden since 2021.)

Sedan hon flyttade har vi inte setts. (Since she moved, we haven't met.)

Note: for a period of time, Swedish uses i, not sedan: Jag har bott här i tre år (I have lived here for three years).

Sen: the short, informal form

In speech, text messages and relaxed writing, Swedes often shorten sedan to sen.

Vi hörs sen! (Talk to you later!)

In job applications, CVs, school work and formal emails, write sedan.

Sen meaning "late"

As an adjective, sen means late. It changes form like other adjectives: sen – sent – sena. You can never replace this one with sedan.

Förlåt, jag är sen! (Sorry, I'm late!)

Tåget är sent. (The train is late – ett tåg.)

Det blev en sen kväll. (It was a late night.)

Practice

1. In a CV: Jag har arbetat som lärare ___ 2019.

sedan – since, formal text.

2. Bussen är tio minuter ___. (The bus is ten minutes late.)

sen – the adjective late (en buss).

3. Vi handlade och ___ lagade vi mat. (…and then we cooked.)

sedan – or sen in a casual text.
