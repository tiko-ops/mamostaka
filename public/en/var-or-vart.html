Var or vart in Swedish?

Short answer

Var = where (location): Var bor du? – Where do you live? Vart = where to (direction): Vart ska du? – Where are you going? Trick: if you could add to in English, write vart.

Location or direction?

English uses where for both, but Swedish has two words:

  • var – where something is or happens
  • vart – where something is going

Var är toaletten? (Where is the toilet?)

Vart går bussen? (Where does the bus go to?)

Vart bor du? (should be: Var bor du?)

The trick: "where to?"

Try adding to in English. If where … to? makes sense, write vart. If not, write var.

You can also think of the answer: här/där (here/there) → var. hit/dit (to here/to there) → vart. The same pattern gives hemma vs hem – see hem or hemma.

Var has other meanings too

  • past tense of vara (to be): Det var kallt. – It was cold.
  • every: var tredje vecka – every third week

In some dialects vart is used as past tense of bli (became). In writing, use blev: Det blev bra.

Practice

1. ___ jobbar du? (Where do you work?)

Var – location.

2. ___ ska ni åka i sommar? (Where are you going this summer?)

Vart – direction (where to).

3. Jag vet inte ___ jag lade nycklarna. (I don't know where I put the keys.)

var – the keys are in a place.
