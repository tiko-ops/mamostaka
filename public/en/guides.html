Common Swedish grammar mistakes

Short guides in English for people learning Swedish – with simple rules, tricks that are easy to remember, and exercises.

  • De or dem in Swedish?

    De = they (the subject). Dem = them (the object, or after a preposition). Both are pronounced dom, which is why even Swedes mix them up. And before an adjective, de means the: de röda skorna.

  • En or ett in Swedish?

    Most Swedish nouns are en-words – roughly three out of four – so en is the best guess. Nouns ending in -ning, -het, -tion and -are are almost always en-words; nouns ending in -ande, -ende and -um are usually ett-words. After ett, the adjective usually gets a -t: ett stort hus.

  • Swedish word order

    In a Swedish main clause the verb always comes second. If you start with something other than the subject, the subject moves after the verb: Idag går jag till jobbet – not Idag jag går. In subordinate clauses, words like inte come before the verb: …att jag inte kan.

  • Compound words in Swedish

    Swedish builds new words by joining words together – and writes them as one word: sjuksköterska (nurse), datorskärm (computer screen). Writing them apart is called särskrivning, and it can change the meaning: sjuk sköterska means a sick nurse.
