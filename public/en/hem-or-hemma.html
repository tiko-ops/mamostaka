Hem or hemma in Swedish?

Short answer

Hem = (to) home, direction: Jag går hem. – I'm going home. Hemma = at home, location: Jag är hemma. – I'm at home. The same pattern: hit/här, dit/där, ut/ute, in/inne, upp/uppe, ner/nere.

Direction or location?

English home can mean both "to home" and "at home". Swedish uses two different words:

Vi åker hem nu. (We're going home now.)

Är du hemma? (Are you at home?)

Jag är hem. (should be: Jag är hemma.)

The same pattern in many words

  • hit (to here) – här (here)
  • dit (to there) – där (there)
  • ut (out) – ute (outside)
  • in (in) – inne (inside)
  • upp (up) – uppe (up there)
  • ner (down) – nere (down there)
  • bort (away) – borta (gone, away)
  • fram (forward) – framme (arrived)

Kom hit! (Come here!)

Barnen leker ute. (The kids are playing outside.)

Vi är framme! (We've arrived!)

The trick: look at the verb

Verbs of movement – gå, åka, komma, springa, flytta – take the direction word. Verbs of being – vara, bo, sitta, stanna – take the location word. It's the same logic as var or vart.

Practice

1. Jag stannar ___ i dag. (I'm staying at home today.)

hemma – location.

2. Kan du komma ___? (Can you come here?)

hit – direction.

3. Det regnar ___. (It's raining outside.)

ute – location.
