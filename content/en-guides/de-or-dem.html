The rule: they = de, them = dem

If you speak English, this one is easy: translate the sentence in your head.

  • de = they – the people or things that do something.
  • dem = them – the people or things something is done to, or after a preposition (till, med, för, av, om…).

De bor i Malmö. (They live in Malmö.)

Jag ringde dem i går. (I called them yesterday.)

Vi åt middag med dem. (We had dinner with them.)

Dem pratar för snabbt. (should be: De pratar…)

Why it's confusing: both are pronounced "dom"

In spoken Swedish, de and dem both sound like dom. You'll also see dom in text messages – but in anything formal (emails, applications, school work) write de or dem.

De before an adjective means "the"

When de stands before an adjective and a plural noun, it's an article meaning the. Then it's always de, never dem.

Jag köpte de röda skorna. (I bought the red shoes.)

Hon pratade med de nya grannarna. (She talked to the new neighbours.)

A trick that works without English: vi or oss

Replace the word with vi (we) or oss (us). If vi fits, write de. If oss fits, write dem.

Practice

1. ___ har en hund. (They have a dog.)

De – subject (they).

2. Kan du hjälpa ___? (Can you help them?)

dem – object (them).

3. Var är ___ nya böckerna? (Where are the new books?)

de – article before an adjective (the).
